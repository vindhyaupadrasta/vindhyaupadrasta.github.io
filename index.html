In my own words

Strategy on one side. Architecture on the other. I stand in the middle.

I'm a problem-untangler at heart. Over 16 years across banking, payments, healthcare, martech, and technology consulting, I've bridged business strategy and modern data architecture, turning complex technology into products people actually value.

My favorite part of the job is talking to customers. I keep asking questions until I understand the real problem underneath the request, because that's where the best products start.

I like owning the whole journey, from the right problem and a multi-year roadmap to launch and real adoption. I've done it with global, cross-functional, multi-vendor teams, including 20+ global PI Planning events, so I know clarity and coordination decide whether big programs ship.

I'm known as the optimizer. I look for the best outcome, and I'm upfront about the trade-offs and big challenges it takes to get there. I'm also organized and direct. I keep plans, dependencies, and decisions visible, and I'm comfortable pushing back, making the call, and saying a polite no when it protects the outcome. I lead with empathy, and I stay clear about what needs to happen.

These days I'm most excited about AI-first products, data governance, and the responsible use of AI. I'm deepening that through Kellogg's Advanced Certification in AI and Product Strategy (Northwestern University).

How I lead

Clear goals, shared playbooks, room to grow.

At Blend360 I built the Product Management Center of Excellence and led a team of 6+ Product Managers. I also led cross-functional, multi-vendor client programs with global teams. This is the approach behind both.

Start with the decision

Before roadmaps and architecture, I ask what decision this product helps someone make, and what it is worth to the business. Everything gets simpler from there.

Playbooks, not heroics

Good product work should be repeatable. I turn what works into reusable playbooks (15+ so far), so teams spend their energy on new problems.

Coach for judgment

I invest in the people on my team: clear feedback, real ownership, and honest conversations about trade-offs. That is how PMs grow into leaders.
